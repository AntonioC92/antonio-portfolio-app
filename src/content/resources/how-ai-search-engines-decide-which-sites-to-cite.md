A prospect asks ChatGPT a question about your industry. It answers confidently, cites three sources, and none of them are you. Your competitor is one of the three. You rank above them on Google.

That gap is showing up in Quora threads and marketing forums constantly right now: people asking how ChatGPT picks which sites to quote, and why a lower-ranked competitor keeps getting the mention. It is a fair question, and it now has a data-backed answer.

## Quick answer: how do AI search engines decide which sites to cite?

- Brand mentions across the web predict citations far better than backlinks do.
- Being crawlable and unblocked to AI bots is a baseline requirement, not an advantage.
- Content structured as direct, standalone answers gets pulled into responses more often.
- Earned coverage (press, reviews, forums) drives the large majority of citations, not owned content.
- Ranking in Google's top 10 still helps, but it explains a shrinking share of what gets cited.

## Brand mentions beat backlinks, by a wide margin

For years, link building was the main lever for search visibility. AI citations run on a different signal.

[Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/) studied 75,000 brands and found that web mentions correlate with AI Overview visibility at 0.664, compared to just 0.218 for backlinks. Brands in the top 25% for mentions averaged 169 AI Overview citations. Brands in the next quartile down averaged 14, a tenfold gap between two groups that were probably running similar SEO playbooks.

The practical read: being talked about, in reviews, comparison posts, local write-ups, and forum answers, now matters more than being linked to. A backlink strategy alone will not close this gap.

## Being crawlable is the floor, not the ceiling

Before any of that matters, an AI system has to be able to reach your content in the first place. Research on citation ranking factors scores URL accessibility, whether a page is crawlable, unblocked, and not paywalled, as one of the single strongest predictors of citation.

That means checking whether GPTBot, ClaudeBot, and PerplexityBot are allowed in your robots.txt, and whether key pages return clean, fast responses. It is unglamorous work, and it sits close to the kind of technical debt we cover in a full [martech audit](/insights/martech-stack-audit). Skip it, and none of the content strategy below gets a chance to work.

## Write passages that can stand alone

AI engines do not read a page top to bottom the way a person does. They pull passages, often a paragraph or two, and drop them into an answer with a citation attached.

Content that leads with a direct answer, uses clear H2 and H3 headings, and includes a short summary near the top gets extracted more reliably. FAQ-style sections get heavy use for the same reason: a clean question-and-answer pair is easy to lift and easy to trust.

This is the same reasoning behind the quick-answer sections at the top of our own articles, including the one you are reading. It is why we brief [AI copy](/insights/briefing-ai-for-marketing-copy) the same way internally: a direct answer first, context after.

## Structured data still earns its keep

Schema markup does not guarantee a citation, but it removes ambiguity that could cost you one. Article, Organization, FAQ, and HowTo schema all give an AI crawler a labeled, machine-readable version of the same information a human reader gets from your layout.

Think of it as translation, not persuasion. A page with clean schema is simply easier for a model to parse correctly and attribute to the right entity, especially when your brand name is common or shares a name with something unrelated.

## Earned coverage does most of the work

A [Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/blog/what-is-ai-reading-may-2026) analysis of what large language models actually cite found that earned media, journalism, reviews, and third-party coverage, accounts for 84% of citations. Paid and advertorial content accounts for roughly 0.3%.

That ratio has held steady across three separate editions of the study since mid-2025. It is not a temporary quirk of one model. Owned content still matters for the reasons above, but it will rarely be the thing that gets quoted back to a prospect. A mention in a trade publication or a detailed customer review usually will.

For a small business, that points toward specific, doable work rather than a vague PR retainer. Answering real questions on forums like Quora, showing up in review platforms relevant to your category, and getting a genuine mention in local or trade press all feed the same signal. None of it requires a large budget, but it does require someone to own it as an ongoing task rather than a one-off campaign.

## Top 10 rankings still help, just less than before

Traditional SEO is not irrelevant here. Ranking in Google's top 10 still correlates with getting cited in AI Overviews, but the relationship is loosening fast.

[Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/) found that only 38% of AI Overview citations now come from pages ranking in the top 10, down from around 76% in mid-2025. Google's system increasingly pulls from expanded sub-queries well outside the original search, which is one reason a page ranking on page two can still end up quoted.

## The gap is widening while most teams aren't watching it

This is not a slow-moving trend. [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/blog/insights/ai-news/chatgpt-referral-traffic-triples/) recorded a 157.7% week-on-week jump in ChatGPT referral traffic after a single product update in May 2026, with homepage-landing referrals nearly doubling as a share of that traffic.

Most small marketing teams still have no line item for this. Citation tracking rarely shows up next to the [KPIs](/insights/marketing-kpis-that-actually-matter) that actually move a monthly dashboard, and it should. If a competitor is quietly compounding citations while you compound backlinks, the gap will show up in pipeline before it shows up in any report you are currently running.

## What to check this quarter

Start with three things: confirm AI crawlers can reach your key pages, audit how often your brand gets mentioned outside your own site versus how often it gets linked, and rewrite your two or three highest-intent pages so the first two sentences answer the reader's question directly.

Add schema to those same pages while you are in there. It is a small technical lift compared with the content and mention work above, and it removes one more reason a model might misattribute or skip your page entirely.
