Clients keep asking whether they should start buying ads inside ChatGPT now that it's actually possible. The honest answer depends on which platform you mean, because the three major AI answer engines have taken three different positions on advertising this year, and none of them give you a mature, predictable channel yet.

We've spent the last few weeks digging through what OpenAI, Google, and Perplexity have each shipped, tested, or abandoned. Here's where things actually stand.

## Quick answer: should you advertise inside AI search right now?

- ChatGPT Ads expanded to seven more Asia-Pacific markets on September 23, 2026, and is now live in more than 60 countries, but most buying still runs through a six-figure minimum commitment or a limited self-serve rollout.
- Early advertiser data is genuinely mixed: some campaigns report click-through rates well below search benchmarks, while OpenAI's own partners cite 3x return on ad spend and mostly new-customer traffic.
- Google's AI Mode ad formats, including Conversational Discovery ads and Highlighted Answers, are mostly still in testing rather than generally available.
- Perplexity pulled ads entirely in February 2026, betting that user trust matters more than ad revenue.
- For most small and mid-size marketing teams, the same budget still goes further on earning [AI citations](/insights/structuring-pages-for-ai-citations) than on buying an unproven placement.

## What's actually live in ChatGPT right now

ChatGPT Ads shows sponsored listings beneath an answer, labeled as sponsored and visually separated from the organic response, according to [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/testing-ads-in-chatgpt/). Targeting runs on conversation topic, chat history, and past ad interactions, and only Free and Go tier users see them at all.

The rollout has moved fast. OpenAI tested ads in the US starting February 9, 2026, expanded to the UK, Mexico, Brazil, Japan, and South Korea by August, and added seven Southeast Asian markets plus Taiwan on [September 23](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-ads-expands-southeast-asia-taiwan/), pushing coverage past 60 countries. Advertising Policies and OpenAI's privacy page confirm advertisers never see individual chats, only aggregate clicks and views.

Access is still uneven. Initial buys reportedly needed a commitment around $200,000 with a roughly $60 CPM, according to [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/chatgpt-ads-will-soon-reach-more-users--proven-results-still-missing). Self-serve Ads Manager access has since widened to India, Europe, the Middle East, and North Africa, but a smaller advertiser testing this on a modest budget is still the exception rather than the rule.

## Why early advertiser results are so mixed

Nobody running ChatGPT ads right now has a clean, settled answer on performance, and the data explains why. Agencies and measurement firms are reporting numbers that swing from disappointing to strong, often for the same ad format in different accounts.

On the weak side: Adthena found one brand's click-through rate at 0.91%, roughly seven times below the 6.4% benchmark for traditional Google search in that sector, and flagged reporting glitches in Ads Manager. SE Ranking's scan of over 50,000 prompts found 14.35% of ads were off-topic, rising past 50% in news and politics categories, and the advertised brand went unmentioned in 96.37% of its own placements.

Agency executives told MediaPost of click-through rates near 0.6%, cost-per-click around $7, zero conversions, and click counts that didn't match their own Google Analytics sessions.

On the strong side: OpenAI says one ecommerce partner saw a 3x return on ad spend over 28 days, and a measurement partner reports more than 80% of ad-driven ChatGPT traffic comes from new customers. OpenAI itself hit a $1 billion annualized revenue run rate in under 200 days, according to [Trending Topics](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/chatgpt-ads-hits-1-billion-run-rate-but-advertisers-are-not-sold/), with tens of thousands of advertisers now buying. That's real volume, even if individual results vary wildly.

The gap between those two pictures comes down mostly to measurement maturity. Buying still runs through spreadsheets, emails, and phone calls for most advertisers, and OpenAI offers little beyond basic clicks and views. If you can't see cost-per-acquisition cleanly, you can't tell a bad week from a bad channel, which is exactly the [attribution gap](/insights/ai-search-attribution-gap) AI search keeps creating for marketers right now.

## Where Google and Perplexity stand

Google took a slower, more cautious path than OpenAI. Its own [Google](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/google-marketing-live-search-ads/) blog post from May 2026 lists Conversational Discovery ads and Highlighted Answers as still in testing, with AI-powered Shopping ads and a Business Agent for Leads format described as coming "in the coming months." A Direct Offers pilot for native checkout has been running since January 2026, but the headline formats most marketers are curious about simply aren't generally available yet.

Perplexity went the opposite direction entirely. The company quietly ended its sponsored content experiment in February 2026 after testing it through 2024 and early 2025. Executives told the Financial Times the risk wasn't performance, it was trust: one leader said in [TechSpot's coverage](https://www.techspot.com/news/111374-perplexity-walks-away-ads-differentiate-openai-google.html) that "the challenge with ads is that a user would just start doubting everything." Perplexity now leans entirely on subscriptions, reporting over 100 million users and roughly $200 million in annualized revenue without a single ad dollar.

Three platforms, three strategies, and none of them converging on a shared standard yet. That's worth remembering before you build a paid AI search line item into next year's plan.

The split also tells you something about how each company sees its own product. OpenAI needs revenue at scale and is moving fast to get it. Google is protecting a search ads business worth far more than any early AI Mode format, so it can afford to test slowly. Perplexity has neither pressure, since its subscription base already funds the company, which is exactly why it could walk away from ads without much risk.

## What this means for your budget right now

If you're running a lean marketing team and weighing ChatGPT Ads against other priorities, the math doesn't favor rushing in. A six-figure entry point, immature reporting, and click-through rates that agencies are openly calling disappointing make this a channel best suited to advertisers who can absorb a genuinely experimental spend this quarter.

The stronger move for most of our clients is still earning placement rather than buying it. Structuring content so answer engines can actually cite it, tracking whether you land a [citation](/insights/ai-citations-vs-mentions) or just a passing mention, and watching your [AI visibility](/insights/tracking-ai-search-visibility) shift across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google month to month costs far less than a six-figure ad test with unproven returns.

That doesn't mean ignoring this space entirely. Self-serve access is widening, OpenAI's own numbers show real scale, and a brand with the budget to run a genuine pilot now could learn things competitors won't know for another year.
