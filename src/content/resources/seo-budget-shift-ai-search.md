Every budget review this year lands on the same question: how much of the SEO line should move to AI search. Marketing leaders are getting pitched a specific range, usually 15 to 30%, and treating it as the answer.

That range comes from aggregate survey data averaged across thousands of companies. Before any budget moves, you need your own signal on whether buyers are actually finding you through AI search, and whether the content you already have is positioned to be cited when they do.

## Quick answer: how much SEO budget should move to AI search in 2026?

- Start by measuring your own AI referral traffic and citation presence rather than applying an industry-wide percentage.
- If AI referral sessions are under 2-3% of organic traffic, a small pilot budget (5-10% of the SEO line) is enough to test the channel properly.
- Fund the pilot from underperforming SEO spend first: low-intent keyword pages, paid link building, generic blog volume. Leave technical SEO and anything already converting alone.
- Increase the allocation only after AI referral sessions start converting and citation share grows month over month. Let those two trends set the timing.
- Keep running core SEO. AI answer engines still pull from indexed, crawlable web content, so weakening your SEO foundation weakens your AI search visibility too.

## Why AI search is pulling budget away from SEO

Budget is moving because buyer behavior already moved. Referral traffic from AI tools like ChatGPT and Gemini has roughly tripled over the past year, and the share of companies with a dedicated AI search budget jumped from 38% to 69% in a single year, according to [SEOFOMO's 2026 survey](https://hub.seofomo.co/surveys/state-ai-search-optimization/).

The buyer-behavior data backs it up. [HubSpot's research](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/ai-search-behavior) found 42% of CRM software buyers used AI search to evaluate vendors, and AI search was the strongest single predictor of purchase intent across the buying journey it measured. That channel is now showing up in the moments that decide which vendors make a shortlist, well before a sales conversation starts.

For a small marketing team, this is the part worth sitting with. Your buyers do not need a large market to have already changed how they research vendors. A handful of category questions asked in ChatGPT or Perplexity each month can matter more to your pipeline than a much larger volume of generic organic traffic.

## Why a blanket percentage is the wrong place to start

Industry guidance clusters around moving 15 to 30% of search budget into AI search, more for AI-exposed sectors like finance, according to [Similarweb's budget guidance](https://www.similarweb.com/blog/marketing/geo/adapt-seo-budget-for-ai-search/). That range is only a starting point. It assumes every company's buyers use AI search at the same rate, and most companies have no idea what their own rate actually is.

A [Quora thread on the topic](https://www.quora.com/Is-SEO-still-worth-investing-in-if-AI-tools-are-changing-search-behavior) makes the point people keep missing: AI answer engines still depend on indexed, crawlable web content to generate their answers. Cutting SEO spend to fund AI search spend can shrink the pool of content an AI engine has to cite you from in the first place.

There is also a sequencing problem with the generic percentage. A company with strong existing SEO and thin AI presence needs a very different first move than a company with weak SEO and no content structured for citation at all. The second company has a content problem before it has a budget problem, and no percentage split fixes that.

## The test to run before moving any budget

Check two numbers before you reallocate anything: what share of your current organic traffic already comes from AI referrals, and whether your brand shows up when a real buyer question gets asked in ChatGPT or Perplexity. Most GA4 setups can isolate AI referral traffic with a simple source filter.

A handful of manual prompt checks against your top three buyer questions will tell you more than any industry benchmark. Run each question once a week for a month and note whether your brand appears, and whether it appears as a link or only as a name.

If that referral share sits under 2-3% and your brand rarely appears in category answers, a full 15-30% reallocation is premature. Run a pilot instead. Take 5-10% of the SEO budget, put it toward the on-page structure covered in [structuring pages](/insights/structuring-pages-for-ai-citations) for citation, and measure the change over a full quarter before committing more.

Three months is the right window because AI answer engines refresh their sources on their own schedule, independent of your reporting calendar. A page restructured this week might not show up in a cited answer for several weeks, so judging the pilot after two or three weeks will read as a false negative.

## Where to pull the pilot budget from

Pull from the SEO line items already underperforming instead of spreading the cut proportionally across the whole budget. Long-tail keyword pages with low intent and near-zero conversion, paid link-building contracts with flat referring-domain growth, and generic blog volume aimed at search rank rather than a buyer question are the usual candidates.

What should stay untouched: technical SEO (site speed, indexation, crawlability), and any content already converting. AI answer engines crawl the same web SEO was built for, so weakening the technical foundation drags both channels down together.

For a team without a dedicated SEO specialist, this audit does not need to be elaborate. An hour with your analytics and your existing content list, sorted by traffic and conversion, is usually enough to find the two or three pieces worth defunding for the pilot.

## When to increase the allocation

Increase spend only after two signals move together: AI referral sessions convert at a rate close to your organic average, and your citation share for buyer-relevant questions is growing quarter over quarter. Track the second number with the approach in [tracking AI search visibility](/insights/tracking-ai-search-visibility), since most standard analytics tools still can't see it directly.

Watch the gap between being mentioned and being linked as a source too. The [difference between citations and mentions](/insights/ai-citations-vs-mentions) is exactly where budget gets wasted: a brand can show up in an AI answer by name and still get zero referral traffic if it is never the cited source. If your pilot is producing mentions without citations, the fix is usually structural, and no amount of added budget solves a structural gap on its own.

Attribution stays the hardest part of this decision, since [most attribution models](/insights/ai-search-attribution-gap) were never built to see AI referral paths clearly. Treat the pilot's raw referral and conversion numbers as directional evidence. Make the next budget call on the trend across the full quarter.

Most teams do not need a 2026 headline percentage. They need three months of their own data, a pilot funded from the SEO spend that was already underperforming, and a clear trigger for when to add more.
