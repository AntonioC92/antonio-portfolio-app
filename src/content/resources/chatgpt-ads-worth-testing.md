ChatGPT stopped being just a place people research your product this month. On September 16, 2026, [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/reimagining-advertising-with-ai/) turned it into a place they can buy from you directly, with a paid ad format built straight into HubSpot and Shopify. If you run marketing for a small team, that changes the question from whether ChatGPT matters to whether it is now a channel worth budget.

## Quick answer: should you test ChatGPT ads yet?

- [Pricing](https://www.webfx.com/blog/ai/chatgpt-ads-cost/) runs roughly $25 to $60 CPM and $3 to $5 CPC in most retail categories, climbing to $8 to $18 CPC in software and finance.
- The $200,000 minimum spend is gone. [Self-serve](https://www.indexlab.ai/guides/chatgpt-ads/costs) Ads Manager opened to all US businesses on May 5, 2026, so a small budget can actually run a test.
- Independent data is mixed. An SE Ranking analysis of 50,000-plus prompts put the mismatch rate between ads and what users actually asked at [14.35%](https://searchengineland.com/study-chatgpt-ads-appear-on-26-of-commercial-prompts-484590).
- Half of users in a [Michigan](https://theconversation.com/you-probably-wouldnt-notice-if-an-ai-chatbot-slipped-ads-into-its-responses-276010) study did not notice a clearly labeled sponsored suggestion was an ad at all.
- Test now if your product fits a conversational buying moment and you already run HubSpot or Shopify. Wait if your margins cannot absorb an experimental spend, given [one advertiser](https://www.outoftheboxadvisors.com/post/we-spent-real-money-on-chatgpt-ads-for-small-business-here-s-the-honest-truth)'s $500 test produced zero leads.

## What OpenAI actually launched

OpenAI rolled out Sponsored Agents on September 16, 2026: a paid format that lets a business's own AI agent join a ChatGPT conversation directly, instead of just showing a link below the answer. HubSpot became OpenAI's first CRM partner, and US Shopify merchants got a dedicated app connecting their product catalog straight to ad campaigns.

A user can now ask ChatGPT about a category, get a sponsored agent from a specific brand inside that same conversation, ask follow-up questions, and click through when ready. International expansion for the Shopify app followed on September 23, in markets where ChatGPT Ads already run.

For a business already running its CRM in HubSpot or its store on Shopify, testing does not require a new tool or a new agency relationship. Distribution now sits inside the tools teams already use for sales and fulfillment as much as marketing, which is the real shift here.

## What ChatGPT ads actually cost

ChatGPT ads price similarly to early paid social. Expect $25 to $60 CPM and $3 to $5 CPC across most retail and ecommerce categories, with software and finance running $8 to $18 CPC because OpenAI runs a second-price auction on advertiser demand.

The pricing history explains why most B2B teams treated the February 2026 launch as a spectator sport. Ads sold at a flat $60 CPM against a $200,000 to $250,000 minimum, a number that ruled out anyone below enterprise scale. April's shift to CPC pricing dropped the floor to $50,000, and May's move to self-serve Ads Manager dropped it again, to [$25 a day](https://ivristech.com/chatgpt-ads-cpc-b2b/) with no account minimum at all.

That last change is the one that matters for a small team. It is what makes a real test possible on a real budget, instead of a Fortune 500 media plan.

Before committing spend, size the test against a new-channel budget line rather than against your best-performing channel's numbers. That framing alone changes what counts as a reasonable early result, and it is worth revisiting how you [budget](/insights/marketing-budget-percentage-of-revenue) for experiments generally before you set the number.

## The relevance problem the early data shows

Early independent data complicates the pitch. SE Ranking studied more than 50,000 commercial prompts and found ChatGPT served ads on 25.94% of them, with 14.35% of those ads unrelated to what the user actually asked.

In categories like news and politics, the mismatch climbed past 50%. Only 3.63% of advertisers who bought an ad also appeared as a cited source in the answer above it, which means a click does not signal that the AI vouched for you first.

The trust problem cuts the other way too. Michigan researchers had 179 people complete everyday tasks with a chatbot that clearly labeled its sponsored suggestions, and half of them did not notice the advertising language at all, even though task performance dropped 3 to 4% when ads were present.

That variance matters more for a small brand than a large one. A recognizable name survives a handful of mismatched impressions without much cost. A business nobody has heard of yet is relying on every single impression to make the right first one.

## What a real test can cost you if it fails

The clearest warning comes from a real advertiser's own test. One coaching business spent $500 on Sponsored Agents, and the budget disappeared in just over an hour. Of the 140 clicks the platform reported, only about 13% actually reached the business's site, and the campaign produced zero leads.

That gap between reported clicks and verified site visits is the number to watch before you commit real budget. Your existing [measurement](/insights/attribution-vs-measurement) setup needs to separate what ChatGPT reports from what your own analytics can confirm, the same reconciliation problem AI chat traffic already creates everywhere else it touches the [buyer journey](/insights/ai-search-attribution-gap).

Almost every new ad platform launches this way, with reported numbers that flatter the platform before its own measurement catches up. Early programmatic display went through the same phase years ago. Trust your own tracking pixel over the dashboard until you have enough volume to see the two numbers move together consistently.

## Who should test now, and who should wait

Test now if your product fits a considered, conversational buying moment where a back-and-forth with an agent genuinely helps, and you already run HubSpot or Shopify so setup cost is near zero. Wait if a few hundred dollars of unverifiable clicks would meaningfully hurt your month.

Run any test the way you would [audit](/insights/martech-stack-audit) a new tool before it earns a permanent budget line: a fixed spend cap, your own tracking on every link, and a decision date before you renew. Treat the first month as a diagnostic run, and set the budget for month two based strictly on what your own tracking confirms.

Keep the first test narrow. Pick one product line or service, write the agent's talking points yourself rather than accepting the default prompt, and check your own analytics against the platform's reported clicks daily for the first week rather than waiting for a month-end report.
